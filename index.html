✨ The Short Vowels (الحَرَكَات - Harakāt)

In Arabic, core letters are consonants or long vowels. Short vowel marks (tashkeel) are placed above or below letters to determine their exact vocalization.

دَ

Fatha (فَتْحَة)

Short "a" sound (like 'u' in 'cup')

A small diagonal slash placed above the letter. Makes an open 'a' sound (e.g. دَ = "Da").

دِ

Kasra (كَسْرَة)

Short "i" sound (like 'i' in 'bit')

A small diagonal slash placed below the letter. Makes a short 'ee/i' sound (e.g. دِ = "Di").

دُ

Damma (ضَمَّة)

Short "u" sound (like 'oo' in 'book')

A miniature loop resembling a tiny Waw placed above the letter. Makes a rounded 'u' sound (e.g. دُ = "Du").

دْ

Sukun (سُكُون)

Zero vowel / Stop

A tiny circle placed above the letter indicating the absence of any vowel. The consonant sound stops cleanly (e.g. دْ = "D").

⚡ Shaddah & Tanwin (Doubling & Nunation)

دّ

Shaddah (شَدَّة)

Consonant Doubling / Emphasis

Looks like a small 'w'. It tells you to pronounce the letter twice with extra strength and duration, holding the consonant before releasing it.

دًا دٍ دٌ

Tanwin (تَنْوِين)

Nunation (-an, -in, -un)

Doubling of the vowel marks at the end of indefinite nouns, adding an 'n' sound: Fathatayn (-an), Kasratayn (-in), and Dammatayn (-un).

☀️ 🌙 Vital Arabic Orthography Rules

☀️ Sun Letters (الحُرُوفُ الشَّمْسِيَّة)

When the definite article Al- (الـ) precedes a Sun letter, the "L" sound assimilates into the letter, which takes a Shaddah (e.g., ash-shams instead of al-shams).

🌙 Moon Letters (الحُرُوفُ القَمَرِيَّة)

When Al- (الـ) precedes a Moon letter, the "L" sound is pronounced clearly and distinctly (e.g., al-qamar).

🔗 The 6 Non-Connecting Letters (حُرُوفُ الفَصْل)

These 6 unique letters connect to the preceding letter (from the right), but never connect to the letter after them (to the left). Any letter following them must start in its isolated or initial form!
